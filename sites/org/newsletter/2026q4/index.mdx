## 👚 Our Bella bodice block now comes with sleeves

Our [Bella block](https://freesewing.eu/designs/bella/) for womenswear is
FreeSewing's second most popular design, with only
[Simon](https://freesewing.eu/designs/simon) proving to be more popular.

So it feels significant that it's now even better, after Jonathan added a
sleeve to the design. That's right, up until recently, the design only
provided the bodice block itself, with the sleeve left as _an exercise for the
reader_ so to speak.

The absence of a sleeve has not proven to be much of a hindrance to its surge
in popularity, so we expect that this will only continue now that sleeves are
included.

## 💬 Self-hosted chat and video conferencing for the FreeSewing community

Eighteen months ago, we announced that we had migrated our backend systems to
Europe, our code repository to Codeberg, and launched [our
forum](https://forum.freesewing.eu/) as an alternative to [our Discord
server](https://discord.freesewing.eu/). All of this in an effort to, as we put
it back then, [put some distance between our users and the new US
administration](https://freesewing.eu/blog/us-and-eu).

Obviously this moment was preceded by a bunch of work to make all this
possible, and that work has continued ever since to deal with the _long tail_
of things that also might need to be reconsidered.

As mentioned, we had already put up our own self-hosted forum at this point to
give people another outlet, which has been a nice addition. But it did not
provide an alternative for real-time chat and video conferencing that were
also provided by Discord.

The video conferencing is particularly relevant because one of the remaining
tasks on our todo list was to ensure that Discord was not load-bearing
infrastructure for the FreeSewing community. Given that we hold [FreeSewing
community calls](https://forum.freesewing.eu/c/hangout/4) every two weeks (feel
free to drop by) and those were hosted on Discord, this means that people who
wanted to partake in those calls did not have an alternative.

So, over the summer, we've set up two new self-hosted services:

- [chat.freesewing.eu](https://chat.freesewing.eu/) is our own real-time chat
  server backed by Matrix
- [meet.freesewing.eu](https://preview.freesewing.eu/hangout) is our own video
  conferencing server backed by Jitsi

This does not invalidate Discord -- or any other platform -- as a place for the
FreeSewing community to congregate. We've always maintained that we consider
any gathering of people under our banner to be part of our community. We just feel
that when it comes to _official_ activity -- such as community calls -- we need
to make sure those places cannot be policed by US-based tech companies.

In other words, we're not in the business of telling people what to do. If you
like Discord, by all means go for it. However, to clarify what you can expect
where, here's the rule of thumb:

- Things will remain how they are: Which is to say, platforms and popularity
  change over time. Go where your friends are, or wherever feels right to you.
- The community call will be (and has been for several weeks) hosted on our
  own Jitsi instance. To join the call, please go [via this
  page](https://preview.freesewing.eu/hangout) which allows you to sign in with
  your FreeSewing credentials. (this is all so new that this page is only
  available via the new site, see further below in this newsletter)
- On [chat.freesewing.eu](https://chat.freesewing.eu/) you will find fewer
  people (it's brand new and few people even know about it) but over time you
  can expect that this will be the more authoritative source of real-time
  communications. To put it differently, we don't expect Matrix to become more
  popular than Discord overnight, but we do foresee it becoming more of the
  nerve center of FreeSewing's inner workings.

## 📷 Showcase uploads are coming back, but it's complicated

Ever since our security audit in Q1 of this year, the ability to submit your
own showcases has been nerfed.
They are now back, but only on [the new version of our
website](https://preview.freesewing.eu/) (more on that below) that we've been
working on since ... checks notes ... the start of June.

The reason this is all taking seemingly forever is that this showcase
submission functionality is an awkward fit for our infrastructure.
Specifically, we need to let people upload images which then need to be
transferred to the hosting service, and we need to capture that input, turn
that into the required `.mdx` files and folders to add the showcase to our
repository, which means handling commits and creating a pull request
programmatically.

It's not that we cannot do that. But the issue is that the FreeSewing backend
is first and foremost designed to handle the data of our users, and all of this
_now go create a pull request_ functionality sits awkwardly in that codebase.

Furthermore, this type of functionality is also subject to significantly more
_churn_ than things like storing measurements or handling authentication.
Migrate image hosting from Cloudflare to self-hosted? Now you need to adapt the
code. Migrate the repository from Github to Codeberg? Now you need to adapt the
code.

Note that we also [publish our backend as a container
image](https://hub.docker.com/r/freesewing/backend) for people who want to run
their local instance of FreeSewing, so we feel a certain responsibility to keep
that image sane, and not make it a convoluted mess that is tightly-coupled to
our own website.

All of this to say that we have decided to strip all of this functionality from
our backend and instead look for a solution that is a better fit for our
needs. A solution that (now) is right under our noses, because in our efforts
to open up our infrastructure and allow more people to keep an eye on
things, we have already set up
[morio.freesewing.eu](https://morio.freesewing.eu/) which is our own
[Morio](https://morio.it/) instance which provides
observability of our infrastructure (in case you're curious about this
choice, Morio is another open source project of mine, so I'm _dogfooding_ it.)

So the new _upload your showcase here_ functionality is powered by Morio's
event-driven automation service (EdA) which itself is an instance of
[Node-RED](https://nodered.org/). This allows us to easily add specific
automations without having to meddle with the codebase of our backend systems.

Going forward, we expect Morio to solve more of these kinds of issues for us.
In the meantime, you can still submit your showcases [on our
forum](https://forum.freesewing.eu/c/showcase/14) or read on to learn about
this new website we've been working on.

## 🎨 Preview our new website before we flip the switch

In June, we set out to port our website from Docusaurus to Vite, in an
effort to make it easier for people to make contributions to our frontend code.

That effort has since [ballooned a
bit](https://codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing/pulls/871) and with it its
estimated end date has slipped.

Originally the plan was to do this _during the summer_ with August 25th
(FreeSewing's birthday) as a loosely penciled in release date. That obviously
didn't happen, and so the end of the quarter -- coinciding with this newsletter
-- was put up as a new target date.

There was some debate about whether or not to release the new site today, but
in the end, we decided against it, instead sticking to our time-honoured
release schedule of _it will be ready when it's ready_. We _feel_ it is close
to ready, bar a few sharp edges still left and right, but our main concern is
the issues we aren't aware of yet.

So if you are curious and want to have a look at what's in store, please do
check it out, and let us know about any issues you can find. This way:
[preview.freesewing.eu](https://preview.freesewing.eu/). Doing so would really
help us out too.

## 🗓️ So much for punctuality?

When we started this newsletter, almost 6 years ago, we promised we
would send you an email once every three months. We've kept that promise,
but now we're struggling to respect the _timing_ aspect of that promise.

You see, over those years, our little newsletter that we used to send to a
handful of people has evolved into something a lot more unwieldy. It's still
our little newsletter, but things have gotten _complicated_. Not only
because we now have more than 70K subscribers, but also because we walked
away from US-based tech companies, which exposes us to plenty of sharp edges.

So last time we sent out the newsletter, Google (Gmail to be precise) felt we
were _sending too much email_, and ended up dropping a lot of our email.

For reasons that would require an extra 500 lines to explain, the most
clear-cut way for us to sidestep this problem is to drip-feed the sending out
of our newsletter emails, which means that you won't all be getting them on the
same day.

We trust you don't mind. And if you did not receive the previous edition of
this newsletter, keep in mind that you can find all our previous newsletter
editions at [freesewing.eu/newsletter](https://freesewing.eu/newsletter/).

## 🤖 This newsletter brought to you by the state of the internet

I sure am happy that Jonathan added that sleeve to Bella because if not, I
would basically have had no _sewing_ content for this newsletter, and I
suspect y'all like to read about that stuff more than about all the tech
stuff.

Trust me, I know. I has dawned on me recently that I have not made any clothes
in more than 2 years, and I am rather sad about that.

Unfortunately, the state of the internet today -- a toxic mix of tech companies
working feverishly on the torment nexus, and a sea of ocean-boiling AI bots
relentlessly trying to extract value from projects like ours -- means that merely
defending FreeSewing and its users from the hellscape that is today's internet has
all but taken over my life.

Since this is the FreeSewing newsletter, I am writing about what is going on with
FreeSewing. And the reality is that most of our time these days is spent
on things that have nothing to do with sewing, or even patterns. It's all just
pushing back against the deathmarch of end-stage capitalism and the grift of
_just one more datacenter bro_.

Yet I am not discouraged. If anything, I find great strength and joy in the
fact that despite all this, FreeSewing can and does persist.

It really is remarkable how far we've come, and I wanted to take a moment to
thank all of you who have helped along the way.

Let's keep going.

joost
